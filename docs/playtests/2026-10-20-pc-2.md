## Recommendations

1. **Prey fill:** don't trim the shelf. In the Kelp and Reef only ~118–161 fish are drawn and
   they read medium. The prey cost ~1.4 ms a frame of script in the Kelp (60 ms/s), which is no
   more than the kelp sway. In the **Trench** they are the largest script cost (105 ms/s,
   ~2.5 ms a frame), so trim there first: the deep fill ×1.9 → ~×1.4 for the Trench and canyon
   zones (`Config/Prey` `fill`). Even that buys ~1 ms a frame, though. Do the two cheaper wins
   first:
   - **Kelp sway:** KelpController moves as much as all the prey (59 ms/s, 34 ms/s of it in
     `BulkMoveTo`). Sway only plants within ~120 studs (was 180), every other frame past ~60.
   - **Shadows in the dark:** the Trench spends **484 of its 1,014 draw calls** on the shadow
     pass under a sun faded to 14%. Prey, forage and sea life already cast none, so turn off
     `Lighting.GlobalShadows` on the client while `OceanController.setDark` is on (and maybe in
     the Vents). The world dressing could also drop `CastShadow` there.
2. **CULL_EVERY** saved little: FPS was within ±3 everywhere but the Trench (+4). The
   prepare-bound render is the bottleneck, so fewer moving parts (sway, built fish) matter more
   than fewer path updates.
3. **Diamond:** it needs a skin-finish change, not more window layer:
   - lower the Diamond skin's metalness / mirror finish so it stops reflecting its
     surroundings (white den, black water);
   - or give it a faint ice-blue glow map (`emissive`) so it holds pale blue in dark water.

   Then re-check in the den and the Reef.
4. **Manta:** works as designed. The 8 s carry makes it feel good even though a manta is slower
   than any fish.
5. **Publishing from Studio:** a publish leaves Studio in the live editing session, and that
   session then blocks the next publish until it's closed. This one hung on a script commit.
   Next round's brief could ask Connor to close the live session first, or the PC session
   could reopen the file in the same window right after publishing.
